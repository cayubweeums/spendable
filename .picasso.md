# spendable design

Confirmed from the Picasso gallery. Sample 1 for content, sample 6 for color and home layout, sample 5 for the payday calendar.

## Fonts
- Display and the big number: Fraunces (italic for the wordmark and page titles)
- Body: Atkinson Hyperlegible
- Figures, kickers, day labels: IBM Plex Mono, tabular nums

## Color (dark only, OKLCH)
- paper `oklch(0.22 0.02 160)` (forest field)
- paper-deep `oklch(0.26 0.025 160)` (payday cell, focus surfaces)
- ink `oklch(0.93 0.018 95)`
- ink-soft `oklch(0.76 0.02 95)`
- rule `oklch(0.36 0.02 160)`
- forest `oklch(0.8 0.09 155)` (the spendable number, links, checks)
- brass `oklch(0.8 0.08 80)` (section kickers, Sync outline)
- debit `oklch(0.78 0.09 35)` (money leaving)

## Layout
- Home: two columns. Left is a right-aligned subtraction (bank lines, bills, card payment, buffer) ending under a rule in the large forest figure and the per-day line. Right holds the lists: Accounts, Charges (rule and monthly controls), Bills.
- Bills: the payday calendar strip from today to payday, then the bill list and the add form.
- The calendar lives only on Bills.
- Rows separated by 1px rules. No cards, no shadows, radius 0.

## Constraints
- No new dependencies. No Inter. No purple. No gradients on surfaces.
- Copy is short. No em dashes. No "let's".
- Balances come from the bank. Nothing on screen edits them.

## What Actual already solved
Categories and payees are a ledger, the same table as charges. Actual Budget already solved a name you edit in the row, a group, a note, hide, a favorite, and merge by ticking rows. Copy that. The first ticked row in the list keeps the name.
Do not add a settings form, a menu library, or budgeted, spent, and balance columns. Those columns are Actual's envelope math. This app does not do that math.
Add a control only for a job that table does not cover. A payee's category is the default for that payee. Rules are the same ledger: one condition, then one change, in order, before or after import. Learn is a check on that ledger. On, saving a category or a payee's category adds a rule. Off, it does not. From copies one field on that charge into notes, date, amount, payee, or cleared. To stays the typed value. Amount does not change the account balance. A split is amount and category lines on that charge. The parts add up to the bank amount. Undo puts the last charge edit back: category, note, tags, and split. A rule or a bill from that save stays. A filter is conditions on the charge list. Every condition has to match. Saving one keeps that stack. The spendable number stays the full answer. Ticked charges take one category, or become copies. The bank amount and the balance stay. Merging two of them keeps a field that was already filled in. A plan is income, a saving, or an expense on a day of the month. It counts until a bank charge matches. The balance stays the bank's. The month line shows under or over that number. It does not change the number. Reset drops the copied charges and balances, pulls them again, and runs the rules. Rules and settings stay. Reports list this month's money in and out by day, plus spending, the net, net worth, and cash flow. The balances stay. Hide replaces the figures on screen. The balances stay. Figures follow a date format, a number format, the first day of the week, and whether cents show. The balances stay. CSV downloads the charges. The balances stay. Import reads a csv, qif, ofx, or qfx file onto one account. Column names, the date format, and the sign are on that form. The balance stays. A charge with the same amount, payee, and date is skipped. A file charge within two days takes the bank date. A YNAB file lands on accounts with the same name. The balance stays. The first password is the admin. Another person gets a separate file and a temporary password. The balance stays. A charge note is a field on the charge, because the bank does not send one. A tag is another label on that charge. The description rule can set one tag. It does not replace tags already there. A rule that sets a tag adds it and leaves the others.
